Oslofjord small-boat harbour survey · method 1.2

How we find and outline marinas, step by step

A walk-through of the method used to map small-boat harbours (småbåthavner) municipality by municipality around the Oslofjord. The worked example is Oslo, around the Bygdøy peninsula: Frognerkilen on the east side and Bestumkilen on the west.

Working CRS EPSG:25833 (ETRS89 / UTM 33N) · Delivery RFC 7946 GeoJSON, WGS84 · Bygdøy ≈ 59.905° N, 10.68° E

About the example. Oslo is queued but has not been analysed yet. The Bygdøy figures on this page are schematic and show how each step works. They are not results. All counts and timings quoted come from municipalities that are finished, mainly Fredrikstad and Råde.

What counts as a harbour

One rule, three outcomes

The survey maps communal berthing facilities, not every private jetty. The threshold is a project rule agreed with the user, not a legal definition.

smabathavnIncluded

A shared facility with at least 10 berths, or a documented marina or boat club with fixed berths.

mulig_smabathavnIncluded, flagged uncertain

Several connected jetties, but the berth count or shared operation is unclear.

excludedLogged, not delivered

Scattered private jetties, slipways, ferry quays, bathing piers, and plans that were never built.

The polygon represents the jetties, their berths and the water between them. It does not cover the whole sheltered bay, the club's land, car parks or winter storage.

The pipeline at a glance

From municipal boundary to approved dataset

The method, step by step

Worked through for Bygdøy

1

Fix the area: official boundary and a tile grid

The municipal boundary comes from Kartverket's Kommuneinfo API and is the only authority on what belongs to the municipality. Search results that merely say "Oslo" do not count. The boundary is reprojected to UTM 33N, so all distances and areas are in metres.

  • Overview tiles: 4 × 4 km with 400 m overlap, both topographic map and aerial image.
  • Detail tiles: 1 km cells where the topographic map shows 2–99 % water, which means the cell contains shoreline. Each is exported 1.1 km wide at 1320 px.
  • The water mask only sets priorities. It never proves there is no harbour.
Bygdøy

Bygdøy is almost entirely shoreline cells, so the peninsula is covered by a ring of roughly a dozen detail tiles. Inland Oslo (Marka, the city centre away from the fjord) produces no detail tiles. Fredrikstad, with its long coast, produced 282.

2

Collect imagery and candidate sources

Two independent searches feed the candidate list: what the images show, and what sources say exists. Neither is trusted alone.

Every image is stored with its georeference

Each export is requested as JSON first, so the server returns the actual extent and size. The image is saved together with that extent, the request URL, the date and a SHA-256 hash. A failed download is logged as "not checked". It is never treated as "no harbour".

GET .../World_Imagery/MapServer/export?bbox=595950,6640950,597050,6642050
    &bboxSR=25833&imageSR=25833&size=1320,1320&format=jpg&f=pjson
→ {"href": ".../_ags_map….jpg", "extent": {...}, "width": 1320, "height": 1320}

The bbox above is an illustrative example. In practice 0.5 m/px exports sometimes returned an empty href or HTTP 500, while about 0.67–0.83 m/px worked reliably.

3

Draft polygons from the pier geometry

Drawing every harbour by hand does not scale: Fredrikstad alone has 735 OSM piers and moles. Since method version 1.2, draft outlines are computed from the pier lines and then judged by eye. Four geometric operations, all in metres:

poly = union(buffer(pier, 12 m) for pier in cluster).buffer(+20).buffer(-20)
poly = poly ∩ water_from_topo_map.buffer(4 m) ∩ municipal_boundary
Bygdøy

In Frognerkilen the long parallel pontoons would form one or a few large clusters. Small single jetties along the Bygdøy shore would each become a cluster of one, and most of those are later excluded. A pier cluster with no jetty geometry in OSM yields no draft at all, which is why step 5 exists.

4

Judge every candidate by eye

Each draft is drawn in red on the best available image, with the OSM piers in yellow and the boundary dashed. Review sheets show nine candidates at a time. Every candidate gets exactly one decision, written to a decisions file with a reason:

inc · keep draftclip · cut with a boxsplit · two operatorsadd · extend by handexc · exclude + reason
  • Count berths from finger piers, mooring patterns and boats. Ten map strokes are not ten berths.
  • Never add up scattered private jetties to pass the threshold.
  • Group parts of one harbour under one harbour_id. Being close together is not proof of a shared operator.
  • A winter image with empty berths does not show an absence of harbour.
5

Sweep every shoreline tile for what OSM missed

All detail tiles are shown as contact sheets with the accepted (red) and rejected (orange) candidates drawn on. Anything that looks like a jetty with boats but has no outline gets zoomed (0.67 m/px) and, if it qualifies, outlined by hand. The outline is set in UTM metres read off a 50 m grid drawn on the zoom image.

x = xmin + (u / W) · (xmax − xmin)        # pixel column u → easting
y = ymax − (v / H) · (ymax − ymin)        # pixel row v    → northing
(use u + 0.5, v + 0.5 for pixel centres; rescale if the image was shown smaller)
Why this matters

In Fredrikstad the sweep found a 15-boat jetty in Ødegårdskilen that had no OSM pier. In Råde the user spotted a harbour I had missed. That led to a fixed rule: before delivery, every OSM pier cluster that is not covered by a polygon must have a logged decision.

A club in Frognerkilen that publishes its berth total gets that number on one row, with scope "whole harbour". A club's postal address in the city is never used to place it on the map.

7

Check that it is right before anyone sees it

A polygon can be geometrically valid and still cover an apartment block. So there are two kinds of checks, and both must pass after the last edit.

Visual overlayThe exported GeoJSON is projected back onto the source image, and every polygon is inspected for land, buildings, car parks, missing piers or the fairway. In Fredrikstad this caught 3 errors.
GeometryValid, closed, non-empty single polygons in [lon, lat] order, with the representative point inside.
Boundary0 m² outside the official boundary. Clipping is flagged.
OverlapNo overlap ≥ 1 m² within the delivery, and none at all with already approved municipalities.
IDs & CSVUnique IDs, no collision with the combined file, and the CSV has identical rows in the same order.
LedgerEvery candidate (included, merged, excluded or unlocated) has a decision and a reason.
8

User approval, then merge as a set

The user reviews the municipality's GeoJSON and approves it. Only then are approval_status and approved_utc set, the municipal extract is written to data/kommuner/…/rev-NN/, and the polygons are appended to the combined file and manifest. A later revision replaces the municipality's set as a whole, so withdrawn IDs cannot linger. Approval does not remove uncertainty: possible harbours stay possible.

Making it faster

How the process can be streamlined and automated further

Ordered roughly by expected gain. Download time and human review dominate today. The geometry is cheap.

Download time

Use cached map tiles and a local OSM extract

Esri's tile cache (/tile/{z}/{y}/{x}) serves pre-rendered tiles far faster than the export endpoint, which renders every request. A Geofabrik norway-latest.osm.pbf filtered locally with osmium removes the Overpass timeouts entirely. Prefetch the next municipality while the current one is reviewed, as done for the Bærum–Oslo batch.

Fewer misses

Add authoritative vector sources

The FKB dataset (where licensed) contains quays, jetties and floating piers. Kartverket's N50 coastline is cleaner than a colour mask. Municipal zoning plans mark existing småbåthavn areas (land-use code 6230) and are available through Geonorge WMS/WFS. These give independent candidates and better clipping than a water mask.

Review time

Detect pontoons and boats automatically

The approved polygons (290 so far) are labelled training data. A segmentation or detection model run on the detail tiles can propose jetties without OSM geometry, count visible boats for the 10-berth test, and rank candidates by confidence. The human then reviews only the uncertain ones.

Review time

Replace the decisions file with a review UI

A small web page showing one candidate at a time, with keyboard shortcuts for include, exclude, split and draw, would write the same decision records with less friction. It could also let the user approve or reject individual polygons instead of whole municipalities.

Quality

Automate the checks that are still visual

Flag polygons whose share of land exceeds a threshold (using FKB or N50 land), polygons far larger than their pier length suggests, and OSM pier clusters left uncovered. These flags would steer the human overlay review instead of replacing it.

Trust

Use dated orthophotos where access allows

Norge i bilder offers dated, higher-resolution orthophotos. With access, capture date and accuracy could move from null to real values, and seasonal ambiguity (empty winter berths) would shrink.

Metadata

Ask the clubs once, centrally

Fixed berths and capacity are the weakest fields. The boating federations (KNBF, OBU) and municipal harbour offices hold member lists. A single structured request would beat scraping dozens of club websites with out-of-date figures.

Reproducibility

Turn the scripts into one parameterised pipeline

The per-municipality scripts are copied and adjusted today. A single tool run as pipeline --kommune 0301, with stages that cache their results and a test run on Råde, would make reruns and revisions trivial. It would also allow measured runtimes and token use per stage.

Limits to keep in mind

What the dataset is not

  • Not legal harbour or property boundaries, and not a register of every private berth.
  • Image capture dates and positional accuracy are unknown. Coordinates have many decimals, but that is not precision.
  • Completeness is not claimed. The ledger records what was checked and what was left open.
  • Older municipalities (Holmestrand, Horten) were mapped before the current rule and have not been redone.
